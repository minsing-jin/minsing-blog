## 📰 Participating in the Korea Research Public Opinion Analyzer Project
We conducted in-depth news analysis utilizing LLMs.
The main feature was building a tool that could analyze public opinion by processing a large volume of news text using LLMs and visualization tools. It was an experience of applying the OOP concepts I had tried in the previous RAGChain project all by myself.
In the later stages, since there was no special evaluation metric for whether the analysis went well or not, struggling through human evaluation of the analysis quality one by one using 100 sample news articles was tough, but quite meaningful.


It was fun to brainstorm and define meaningful analysis with T-hyung and Dr. Cheol while researching, experimenting with, and analyzing news articles and comments separately.
Previously, I thought LLMs were almighty AIs that could handle anything, but upon using them, I found areas that needed improvement and aspects that were difficult to replace humans with. First, since answers lacked consistency, exception handling and prompt engineering were needed to ensure consistency in analysis and answers. Second, it was still difficult to trust the content judged and analyzed by LLMs during analysis, and there was the difficulty of lacking an appropriate evaluation metric to determine whether an analysis was good or bad. Third, creating train sets, test sets, and correct-answer datasets to judge answer quality required making various datasets for analysis, which became a bottleneck in terms of time, leading us to push it to the next step.
Consequently, at this point, I began to see LLMs as auxiliary tools to reduce human simple labor.



#### 💪 Before & After
- Meaningful text analysis, meaningful visualization, and utilizing LLMs deeply for analysis
- Utilizing LLMs deeply within the domain of text mining
- Experience building architectures on my own and running experiments
- The thoughts and solutions we melted into the work actually being utilized by companies

# 🥳 Realizations of the Year



### Better together than alone; if doing it together, the person you do it with matters
The time and energy spent face-to-face with your collaborator are important.


### If ideas don't come, pick a theme and just start
There is no such thing as a perfect idea. Let's just start.


### When receiving information or communicating, grasp the core
My weakness was trying to remember every single peripheral detail, which blurred the core. This is a bad habit of mine carried over from the past. Core first, then peripherals through repetition.


### You only know by trying. Let's think while running
I started viewing work from the perspective of how to finish rather than what to start.
The hardest part when reading, communicating with people, or working on something new and unfamiliar was my weakness of not asking out of fear of not knowing, and trying to memorize everything. Moreover, by taking down every single note without knowing what was important, or interpreting things my own way, I would flounder and end up repeating work two or three times. I was trying to solve inefficiency with blind diligence. Or, not really knowing the core of the conversation because it was difficult, I acted like a troll by asking the same questions repeatedly.
There were two most important lessons: first, always keep the core in mind, and second, if you don't know, ask until you know.


### Not looking for or asking for a faster, more efficient, and hacky path, and instead proceeding with inefficient methods under the excuse of blind diligence
- This is my learning problem so far.


### When rushed for time, it's hard to produce good code. (For me)
- In code that doesn't consider reusability, when a client requests changes, you're stuck fixing it for 65 million years.


### Need practice saying no


### Lack of open-mindedness
I tended to lack open-mindedness. There was a confusion between having a blurred subjectivity—listening to others' opinions and accepting them well—and being open-minded. In fields I don't know well, my subjectivity tends to blur; how should I solve this?
The realization: as soon as you don't know, ask until you know, and look it up. Ask and research until you are certain, as much as you possibly can. How do you know whether you know or don't know about something you're unfamiliar with? In my situation, first...


### Helping others is more advantageous; the perspective of finding win-win points with what I can do


### There is a time lag before deliverables shine
Just like the CSAT benchmark, the results don't always shine immediately; you wait a bit until the right timing aligns.

### Making book reading a habit
I developed my own criteria for selecting books.
I choose and read books as a process of resolving dilemmas arising in life. Like a mind map, keyword dilemmas about my life branch out in my head, and books act as nodes to resolve them. Then, books branch out further into new dilemmas that arise after reading, and I pick up another book to solve those dilemmas.
Second, I pick up books not for methodologies and prescriptive instructions, but to learn *how to think*. Most self-help books gently stimulate people's deficiencies with clever rhetoric presenting their own methods and worldviews to offer solutions. However, they lack substance, or tell stories so obvious that they fail to solve my problems, or I've lost direction following them strictly more than once or twice. The biggest problem is that the method suggested by someone is the most suitable method for that author's life, decision-making process, and circumstances. Therefore, just as everyone's lived experiences and circumstances differ, the author's method often fails to greatly help my self-development. Ultimately, one must selectively choose and adapt the author's thoughts and methodologies to fit one's own situation. This was inspired by the idea in the book *Uncopierable*, which states that large enterprises cannot beat small companies that have built ideas and know-how through continuous problem-solving simply by copying ideas. Because of this, I decided to be wary of books where authors merely present methods. Afterward, I view and select books from the perspective of looking at people's stories and learning how they think, like *As a Man Thinketh* or *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant*. I think about: What kind of mindset does this person use to contemplate and make choices? What are their criteria for decision-making?
What I felt while reading books


### You must start even before fear arises
Decide within 10 minutes. If fear is large, you start pondering whether to do it or not and look for reasons why it won't work. Since there are countless reasons why it won't work, let's just start and think later. Don't spend too much time on decisions.


### Being rich isn't everything. Is being rich really what I want?
There's this self-help cliché that you must become rich. My approach, however, was to first raise my own market value. Then, I wanted to do what I love, figure out what I like and what I'm good at, and earn just enough money to get by along the way. If there is something you're good at, and if it's aligned with your passion (making a hobby your career), money will naturally follow. So, how much is "just enough money"?
There is no problem in this world that cannot be solved. There is only a shortage of time for contemplation and resources.


### Shortcomings in ability or unsolved problems stem from a lack of thinking time and brain resources. It is never because of low intelligence.
To solve these problems, everyone has their own mindset, allocated brain resources, and experiences. In the past, I used to think of software development as the realm of geniuses. However, due to constraints and responsibilities in daily life (work, studies), I simply didn't have enough time to ponder, and my brain resources weren't sufficiently allocated to solving the problem. This can be addressed through daily retrospectives, and securing that time for retrospectives can also be solved systematically.
Ultimately, the reason I fail at something is often a system issue rather than just a lack of understanding.


### Is it a world where we can survive just by coding?
I don't think we can survive by coding alone. The barrier to entry for development is gradually lowering, and unless it's high-tech, truly core technology, the technology people will use won't need insane performance—just adequate performance.


### The necessity of exercise

I learned that exercise has a massive influence on guiding one's train of thought in a positive direction. A healthy mind resides in a healthy body. Mental resilience increases, and when your thoughts become too cluttered, you can sort them out.

When tasks are assigned, they aren't given with the intention that you pull all-nighters, and that should be the underlying intent.


### Sheer effort alone doesn't solve everything. Let's build a system that enables repetitive habits.
During a book club meeting, the topic that came up with the hardest difficulty was how hard it is to maintain consistency, effort, and habits. In past mentoring sessions and discussions with acquaintances, people often talk about how hard it is to live diligently. Conversely, I didn't understand this because I used to work and study ignorantly without rest, increasing inefficiency no matter what. This later turned out to be poison for me. It was just fake busyness. You can do things while resting enough. No, you *must* do them while taking breaks and resting. Instead, it needs to be tailored to each person's habits, tendencies, and circumstances.


### There is no such thing as a perfect choice. Make a choice and make that choice the best one.
This became my best realization of the year, which happened during a meal with Woong-i hyung and Dr. Cheol at the beginning of the semester.


### A succession of problem-solving
Life is a succession of problem-solving. To solve problems, how can we ask the right questions?


### I need to write periodically. And those writings shouldn't be based on something I've prepared elsewhere, but should organize thoughts that pop up and are observed right now.


### Break down tasks as much as possible to create small achievements
This is where I felt the importance of retrospectives and journaling the most. I always made plans and failed to meet them within the set time. I used to brush this off, attributing it to a lack of skill, retreating into lowered self-confidence. However, as I started writing a journal and looking back on my days, I realized that truly, small achievements are crucial.


### A person cannot handle more than two big tasks in a day.
I feel like I always set unreasonable goals. Human cognitive limits exist, yet I used to set at least two major tasks like development concurrently and often ended up chasing two hares and catching neither. If I delayed retrospectives under the excuse of being busy, I would end up piling more than two capability-exceeding tasks onto a single day. Consequently, due to the Zeigarnik effect in my brain, I couldn't concentrate on a single thing, bouncing back and forth without being able to focus on one task in the allotted time, ultimately ending the day having accomplished nothing.


### We need a way to speak our minds unfiltered, even if we might be wrong
I realized this through exactly two incidents. First, communicating with Dr. Cheol; second, an interview with a reporter. I need self-confidence. If I don't know something, let's honestly admit I don't know. Or, it's necessary to confidently blurt out something wrong and get it corrected. That way, a mentor can catch the flawed parts or necessary points to provide direction and correction for the task at hand.
In the second incident, the interview with a reporter, strangely enough, despite it being something I created myself, I couldn't explain everything in front of the reporter. When I explain an idea—even if it's a 100-value idea that I've actually researched and pondered to a level of 100—in front of others, thoughts about potential counterarguments mix in out of fear of being wrong, causing cognitive overload. As a result, I end up sharing only 10 or even negative amounts of information. In my situation, I need to speak logically, confidently, and with grounds, treating what I say as the right answer without filtering it through my brain. Otherwise, the outcome of my achievements becomes negative. Taking the plunge and trying things out is what matters.


### My maximum daily concentration span is 4 hours.
This assumes I wake up, read a book to wake up my brain, break down clear tasks within available time resources, and have a clear goal. If this assumption isn't met, considerable inefficiency arises, or I fail to complete the goal and lose focus on a single task due to the Zeigarnik effect of competing tasks.

Even during a liberal arts entrepreneurship class, understandings of tasks differed.
