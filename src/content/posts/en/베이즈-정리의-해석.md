# Key Points
This is the author's interpretation, so please point out any mistakes!


- Probability from a Bayesian perspective: a concept of **predicting** future 'possibilities,' that is, future uncertainty, based on existing evidence (clues that have already occurred, expressed as probabilities) and a hypothesis(). (Induction)
-> Existing: **interpreting** phenomena based on the frequency of observations for specific events (deduction)


- Evidence: new information, a **clue**
- Hypothesis(prior, prior probability): a claim that an event has occurred
- posterior(posterior probability): the probability that H is updated when E (new information) comes in (this is used to predict the future)


-> Confidence in my claim before and after observing the evidence


![](/images/velog/a4aa9a5c83165e15.png)


# Definition of Bayes' Theorem
- Bayes' theorem: a method of **updating** the confidence in a claim that an event has occurred (the possibility of the future being predicted) based on new information
