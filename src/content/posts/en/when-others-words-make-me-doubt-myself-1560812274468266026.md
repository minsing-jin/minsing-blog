# When Someone's Words Make Me Doubt Myself

Someone else's words can make me question myself. I may wonder whether I am mistaken or whether I am creating a problem simply because I do not know enough. But before accepting that doubt, I need to examine whether being pushed to distrust myself is healthy communication at all.

## Do Not Treat Emotion as Evidence

When someone creates guilt, shame, or anxiety to shake my judgment, I can pause and question it. I need to distinguish between an emotion that helps me look at the facts and one that keeps me from making a reasonable request or speaking for myself.

## I Cannot Begin Knowing Everything

I cannot begin with complete knowledge. Not knowing something does not mean I lose the right to ask for what is reasonable or to say what I need to say. I can make my own choices as long as I take responsibility for their consequences.

## Change the Direction of Doubt

Instead of endlessly doubting myself because of someone else's words, I can look at what those words make me feel and what choices they are preventing. I need to judge again through the facts I know and the standards I want to protect, rather than accepting someone else's judgment as my own.
